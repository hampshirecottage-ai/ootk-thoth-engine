What it is for

I made this to do the slow part of a Thoth reading for me: shuffling, looking up every correspondence and scoring how the cards interact. It writes all of that up as a prompt, and your AI does the interpreting.

It is for studying the Thoth deck and its correspondences, for reflection, and for giving an AI the same careful groundwork every time instead of a bare list of card names.

Please don’t use it for decisions about health, money, law or safety. A reading, and whatever an AI makes of it, is something to think with, not advice or a forecast.

How a reading is made
  1. Shuffle. Your seed is turned into a number (SHA-256) that shuffles the 78 cards with Python’s standard shuffler. The same seed always gives the same order, which is what lets you repeat or share a reading. A blank seed gets a random six-digit number. The shuffle is ordinary software: nothing outside the seed chooses the cards.
  2. Draw. Cards are dealt from the top into the spread’s positions. For the 15-card heap and the full Opening of the Key, your significator is placed first. The full Opening of the Key reshuffles the whole deck for each operation, as the Golden Dawn method does, so a card can fall again in a house, sign or decan. For a seeded draw the report also says where your significator falls in each operation when the whole deck is laid out as Book T lays it out: which of the four I H V H packs, then which house, sign and decan pack.
  3. Look up. Each card’s element, sign, planet or decan, Hebrew letter, place on the Tree of Life, colour scales and the rest come from the Book T and Liber 777 tables, under the mapping system you chose (Thoth by default). Only a few of these are calculated with; the rest are handed to your AI as they are. How each one is used.
  4. Score. Neighbouring cards get a Book T elemental dignity. Neighbours are the next card along; in the 15-card heap, the Celtic Cross, the Relationship spread and two rows of three they are the named pairs (in the Celtic Cross: 1 with 2, 3 with 4, 5 with 6, the staff from 7 up to 10, and 1 with 10), and on the Tree of Life the Sephiroth a path joins:
    ElementsScore
    Same element+2
    Friendly (for example Fire and Air)+1
    Contrary (Fire and Water, Air and Earth)−2
    Spirit on either side (neutral)0
    Cards are also related by where they sit in the layout; on the wheels of houses, signs and decans that means astrological aspects, counted within set orbs. These aspects belong to the layout, so they are the same in every reading of a spread; only the cards at their two ends change. The prompt lists only the oppositions unless you add every aspect. The report adds the elemental balance and where the cards fall on the Cube of Space; Platonic solids are an extra layer you can add.
  5. Choose a lens. The framework is the overall story your AI is asked to read the cards as. It sets one line of the prompt and changes nothing else: the cards, scores and calculations stay the same. There are four:
    • Something coming into being (Divine Light Flow): energy coming down from Kether, the divine, into Malkuth, the material world.
    • The soul before birth (Soul Formation): the soul taking shape before birth.
    • Everyday growth (Life Path): growth through everyday life. This is the default.
    • Letting go and returning (Post-Mortem Return): the climb back up the Tree, as in the Book of the Dead.
    On Auto, each card that has a place of its own on the Tree of Life is given a height: a pip its Sephira (Ace in Kether, 10 in Malkuth), a Trump the midpoint of the two Sephiroth its path joins. Court cards have no place of their own and are left out. With at least six such cards, Auto checks whether they run steadily down towards Malkuth in position order (Divine Light Flow) or up towards Kether (Post-Mortem Return); the bar is set so a random shuffle passes it only about one time in ten. Otherwise, and in every spread of fewer than six cards, it uses Life Path. Auto never picks Soul Formation, which has no pattern in the cards to test, so choose it yourself if you want it. The report says which lens was used, and why.
  6. Write the prompt. Section 1 lists the cards, and the sections after it hold the calculations. The last section asks your AI to relate them to your question, using only the data given, to name the outcome they lean toward as a likely possibility, not a certainty, leaving the decision to you, and to end with a recap in plain words. It also explains what the scores mean.

The code, tables and tests are all open source, so every step above can be checked.

How the engine uses the correspondences

Every card carries many correspondences, but the engine treats them in three different ways. A few are calculated with, so they change the scores and checks in the report. Most are looked up and passed to your AI as they are. And two things come from your birth date, if you give one. The “Parts of the AI prompt” switches decide what your AI is shown; they never change the cards drawn or the calculations.

Open any correspondence below to see, step by step, what the code does with it for each card drawn.

Calculated with

Element Scores each pair of neighbouring cards and counts the elements across the spread.
  1. Each card gets one element. A small or court card takes its suit’s: Wands Fire, Cups Water, Swords Air, Disks Earth. A Trump takes a fixed Thoth element: a sign Trump the element of its sign (the Emperor Fire, by Aries); a planetary Trump the element of one of its planet’s signs, the one named on its card (the Empress is Earth by Taurus; Libra would have made her Air); and the Fool, the Hanged Man and the Aeon are Air, Water and Fire themselves. The prompt prints this choice beside each Trump.
  2. The elements are counted. Fire, Water, Air and Earth are added up across the draw. Trumps on the letter Shin, or attributed to Spirit, are named as carrying Spirit too, but they still score with their main element.
  3. Neighbours are paired. Usually that is each card with the next one. On a full house, sign or decan wheel the last card also neighbours the first. A Tree of Life spread pairs the Sephiroth that a path joins. A heap or the Celtic Cross pairs its named pairs. In Book T’s own Opening of the Key, the cards either side of the Significator are paired, then the two outside them, and so on. A pair never crosses from one operation into the next.
  4. Each pair is scored. Same element +2 (strong); friendly elements +1; contrary elements −2 (ill-dignified). Fire and Water are contrary, and so are Air and Earth; every other mix is friendly, as Book T has it. A card with no element (Spirit) scores 0 with anything.
  5. Each card is judged. The scores of every pair a card is in are added up. Zero or more and the card is drawn as well dignified on the card map; below zero, ill-dignified. A card with no neighbour counts as well dignified.
Sign, planet and decan Checks whether a card landed in its own place, rates a small card’s planet, and gives the Sun and Moon.
  1. Each card’s own place is read from its attribution. A small card from 2 to 10 owns its decan and that decan’s sign (“Mars in Aries” is the 2 of Wands: Aries, first decan; 3 and 4 are the second and third). A Knight, Queen or Prince owns the sign it holds two of its three decans in (20° Scorpio to 20° Sagittarius gives Sagittarius). A sign Trump owns its sign. Aces, Princesses and the planet and element Trumps have no own place.
  2. The Own-Place Check. Only on a full wheel: the 12 houses, the 12 signs or the 36 decans. The 1st house goes with Aries and so on round to the 12th with Pisces. Each card that fell in its own house, sign or decan is listed.
  3. The odds. The engine works out exactly how often that many cards or more land at home when a wheel is dealt at random from all 78 cards, with the number you would expect by chance. A count with a chance of 5% or less is marked unusual. The odds are the same in every reading; only the count changes.
  4. Planet strength. For a small card whose attribution is a planet in a sign, the classical dignity is added: rules the sign or exalted (strong), in detriment (weakened), or in fall (weakest). The 3 of Wands, the Sun in Aries, is exalted. Most cards have none, and then nothing is added.
  5. The Sun and Moon. From the moment you draw, the engine works out where the Sun and Moon stand in the tropical zodiac (to about 0.02° and 0.1°), names the decan and its small card for each, and gives the Moon’s phase. This is context only and changes no score.
Position in the layout Gives the aspects between positions; they belong to the spread, not the cards.
  1. Every position has a place on the spread’s drawing. The engine takes the middle of the drawing and measures the angle of each position around it.
  2. On a wheel (houses, signs, decans) every two positions are checked for an exact aspect, within 1°: opposition (180°, −1), square (90°, −2), trine (120°, +2) or sextile (60°, +1). Each such pair also shows the elemental score of the two cards that fell there, which is the only part that changes between readings. By default only the oppositions are shown; “Every wheel aspect” adds the rest.
  3. On other drawn spreads each position is compared with the next, with wider allowances because the drawings are only roughly on the angles: conjunction (0°, within 15°, +2), sextile (within 10°, +1), square (within 10°, −2), trine (within 10°, +2), quincunx (150°, within 5°, −1) and opposition (within 15°, −1). Anything else is unaspected (0). A card at the very middle has no angle and is left out.
  4. Heaps, the Celtic Cross, the Relationship spread and two rows of three make no angles, so they get no aspects: their pairs are scored only by element.
Place on the Tree of Life Used by the Auto lens to see whether the cards run down the Tree or up it.
  1. Each card gets a height on the Tree. A small card sits on its Sephira, numbered from Kether 1 to Malkuth 10 (a 3 is Binah, 3). A Trump sits on its path, halfway between the two Sephiroth it joins (the Fool’s path, Kether to Chokmah, gives 1.5). Court cards have no place of their own on the Tree and are left out.
  2. The trend is measured. With the cards in position order, the engine measures how steadily the height rises or falls (a correlation, r), and weighs it by how many cards there are (z = r × √n).
  3. The lens is chosen. Fewer than 6 cards with a height: Life Path, the default. z of 1.64 or more (heading down to Malkuth): Divine Light Flow. z of −1.64 or less (climbing to Kether): Post-Mortem Return. Anything between: Life Path. A random shuffle passes the test about 10% of the time, 5% each way. Soul Formation is never picked automatically; you can choose it.
  4. The prompt says why. It gives the numbers, so the lens never comes without its reason. A lens you pick yourself skips all of this.
Card numbers and ranks Book T’s counts, and the counting steps in the Opening of the Key.
  1. The counts. In each operation of three cards or more, the engine counts the Trumps, court cards and Aces, and the cards of each suit (small and court cards). Any rank that turns up three or four times is named, with its suits: “Three Queens (Wands, Cups, Disks)”.
  2. The counting number. In Book T’s Opening of the Key each card counts on by a set number, itself included: Aces 11; small cards their own number; Knights, Queens and Princes 4; Princesses 7; Trumps 3 for the elements, 9 for the planets, 12 for the signs.
  3. The count. Starting on the Significator, the engine counts round its pile in the direction the Significator faces on the Thoth card, then counts on again from each card it lands on. It stops on reaching a card already counted. The cards it landed on, in order, are the story.
  4. The pairs. Then the cards either side of the Significator are paired, then those outside them, and so on round the pile; a card left over is read on its own.
Hebrew letter values Added up across the Trumps and court cards drawn (gematria).
  1. Each letter has its standard number, from Aleph 1 to Tav 400.
  2. Which cards have one. A Trump takes the value of its path’s letter. A court card takes the letter it gets through its sign (a Princess through her element). Small cards have no letter of their own, so no value.
  3. The sum. The values are added up for each operation and shown as a plain sum, with no meaning given to it. Off unless you add “Gematria”.

Looked up and handed to your AI

These are named in the prompt for each card, with no score attached. A code check shows most of them restate three facts about the card: its element, its place on the Tree (with its letter), and its sign or planet. They are the same facts in different languages, offered to your AI as Thoth readers use them.

Hebrew letter

A Trump shows the letter of its path in the mapping system you chose (Thoth by default). A court card shows the letter it takes through its sign, or a Princess through her element. A small card has no letter: it shows the name of its Sephira instead. “Hebrew letters in other systems” adds the Golden Dawn and French letters beside it.

Path or Sephira, colours and Yetziratic title

Each card is shown on its path or Sephira. Its four Liber 777 colour scales (King, Queen, Prince, Princess) and its Yetziratic title are listed once per row of 777, with the positions that use it, so a long spread does not repeat them. Under Crowley’s swap the Emperor and the Star take their colours from their sign’s row and their title from the path they are shown on, and the table says which.

Court card element

Each court card is shown as an element of an element: the rank’s element of the suit’s (Knight Fire, Queen Water, Prince Air, Princess Earth), so the Queen of Wands is Water of Fire. Its span of the zodiac is given beside it.

Cube of Space and Platonic solid

A Trump is placed on the Cube of Space by its letter: a mother letter is an axis, a double letter a face or the centre, a simple letter an edge. Small cards sit on a Sephira, not on the cube. Every card takes the solid of its element (Fire the tetrahedron, Water the icosahedron, Air the octahedron, Earth the cube), and the seven planetary Trumps the dodecahedron. Neighbouring cards whose solids are duals are noted. The solids and the cube counts are off unless you add them. See Letters and solids in space.

Geomantic figure

A Trump that carries a zodiac sign is given the Golden Dawn’s figure for that sign, from Liber 777 (Aries Puer, Taurus Amissio and so on). Planet and element Trumps, and all other cards, have none.

Golden Dawn titles

Each card’s first Golden Dawn title is given: the small cards’ from Book T, without “Lord of” (the 4 of Swords is Rest from Strife); the courts’ from Book T; the Trumps’ from Liber 777. Aces have none, as their attribution already is their title.

Gods of the path

Each Trump is given two columns of Liber 777 for its path: the Egyptian gods and the Greek gods. Under the swap the Emperor and the Star take the gods of their sign’s row. Off unless you add “Gods”.

Angels and dates of the decan

Each small card from 2 to 10 rules a decan of ten degrees. It is given the two angels of the Shem ha-Mephorash that rule its two halves, counted from 0° Leo as Book T does (the 5 of Wands has Vehuiah and Yeliel), and the dates the Sun is in that decan in the year of your reading, worked out from the Sun’s position. The planet that rules the day of the reading is given too. Off unless you add them.

I Ching hexagram

Each court card is given Crowley’s hexagram: the trigram of the rank’s element above the trigram of the suit’s (Fire Chên, Water Tui, Air Sun, Earth Kên). Off unless you add “Court card hexagrams”.

From your birth date

A significator Changes the reading itself.
  1. The card. Each Knight, Queen and Prince rules about a month of the zodiac, from 21° of one sign to 20° of the next, as Book T gives them. Your browser finds the one that holds your birthday. Princesses rule quarters of the sky, not dates, so this never gives a Princess.
  2. Where it goes. On spreads with a significator position, that card is put first and taken out of the first operation’s deck, so it cannot be drawn twice there.
  3. What it changes. Each later operation reshuffles the whole deck, so the Significator can fall anywhere. The engine says where it fell in each: which of the four packs, or which house, sign or decan. In Book T’s Opening of the Key the counting and pairing start from it.
Birth and year cards Context only: they change no card and no score.
  1. Birth card. Month + day + year of birth are added (14 July 1983: 7 + 14 + 1983 = 2004), then the digits of the total, until the number is 22 or less (2 + 0 + 0 + 4 = 6, The Lovers). 22 stands for The Fool. While it is 10 or more its digits give the next card too (19 The Sun, then 10 Fortune, then 1 The Magus).
  2. Year card. The same sum with the year of your latest birthday in place of your birth year.
  3. In the prompt. Both cards are named, the prompt says whether either was drawn, and tells your AI they are context only. See Birth cards and year cards.

Either way, the date is worked out in your browser and never sent: only the card it gives is.

What the tests show

Each reading hands your AI many layers of correspondence: elements, astrology, Hebrew letters, the Tree of Life and more. Two kinds of test check them. The first checks the tables against each other; the second checks which layers an AI actually uses.

The tables agree

Most layers restate three facts about a card: its element, its place on the Tree of Life (with its Hebrew letter), and its sign or planet. A code check compares every layer with those three for every card, and all 264 checks pass. So the layers never contradict each other; they say the same thing in different languages.

What an AI leans on

In November 2026 the same 8 readings were sent to Claude Sonnet 384 times: once with everything on, once with each part switched off in turn, and once with the correspondences deliberately scrambled between cards. Each version was asked three times, so the normal wobble between two answers to the same prompt could be measured first.

  • Astrology does most of the work. An answer mentions signs, planets or decans about 28 times. Aspects between cards come next (about 10), then Hebrew letters (6), the Tree of Life (3) and decan dates (2).
  • Some parts are barely used. Angels, gods, geomancy and the Golden Dawn titles are hardly ever mentioned.
  • Some parts don’t change the conclusion. With the angels or the decan dates switched off, the answer leaned the same way (go ahead or wait) as often as two answers with everything on: about 83% of the time.
  • Some parts do. With the dignities, the Tree of Life or the geometry switched off, the lean matched only about 60% of the time. Geomancy showed the same drop, though it is barely mentioned, so part of that drop is simply the prompt changing.
  • Wrong data gets noticed, but the cards still lead. With scrambled correspondences, answers flagged a mismatch eight times as often (1.6 per answer, against 0.2), yet still leaned the same way 72% of the time. The AI’s conclusion rests mostly on which cards fell where.
  • Birth cards nudge it a little. In a separate run of 24 answers, adding a birth date (and so a birth card and year card) to the same readings, the AI mentioned them about 1.6 times an answer and leaned the same way 68% of the time, against 83% for a plain repeat. That gap is under the 20 points this sample needs to be firm.
  • A smaller AI is too unsteady to tell. Claude Haiku gave two answers to the same prompt that agreed on the lean only 46% of the time, so no part stood out from that noise.

Above all of these sits which cards fell where. With every correspondence scrambled, the AI still reached the same conclusion 72% of the time. Choosing the significator by birth date was not tested on its own: it changes which card stands for you, so it changes the reading itself rather than adding a part. Only Claude models were tested, mentions are counted by keyword, and none of this says whether a reading comes true.

What this means: the main parts stay on by default. The angels, decan dates, gods, hexagrams and gematria, along with layers that only repeat another part (Platonic solids, Cube of Space counts, every wheel aspect, the letters in other systems, the statistics), are extra layers, off unless you add them under “Parts of the AI prompt”. The sample is small (24 answers per part), so differences under about 20 points are not firm. And these tests show what an AI uses, not whether a reading comes true. That is a separate question this site doesn’t answer.

Letters and solids in space

Rather than treating the Hebrew alphabet as an abstract list or the Platonic solids as isolated geometry, the report places both in three-dimensional space with two models: the Cube of Space from the Sefer Yetzirah, and polyhedral dual inversions.

  • The Cube of Space. The three mother letters are its axes, the seven double letters its six faces and its centre, and the twelve simple letters its twelve edges, in Paul Case’s layout. Each Major sits where its letter sits; court cards and pips are linked through their sign. Crowley’s swap of Tzaddi and Heh moves letters, not signs, so the Emperor stays on the Aries edge and the Star on the Aquarius edge, where the Sefer Yetzirah puts them.
  • Dual inversions. Each card takes the Platonic solid of its element: Fire the tetrahedron, Water the icosahedron, Air the octahedron, Earth the cube. The seven planetary Majors keep the dodecahedron. Put a corner at the centre of every face of one solid and the corners make its dual: the cube and the octahedron, the dodecahedron and the icosahedron, and the tetrahedron, which is its own dual. The cube’s dual is where the two models meet: its six corners are the centres of the Cube of Space’s six faces, the six directions held by the double letters, with Tav at the centre both solids share. The report notes each pair of neighbouring cards whose solids are duals.

Card maps draws both models and marks where any card sits on them.

The Book T Opening of the Key

The other Opening of the Key spreads lay out a fixed number of cards. The Book T spread follows the method as the Golden Dawn’s Book T gives it (printed in Crowley’s Equinox I(8)): five operations, each from a fresh shuffle of the whole deck, and in each only the pile the significator falls in is read.

  1. The four packs. The deck is cut into four packs, Yod, Heh, Vau and Heh final: work, love, trouble and money.
  2. The twelve houses and the twelve signs. The deck is dealt into twelve stacks, once for the houses and once for the signs.
  3. The ring of 36. The significator and the 36 cards after it in the deck form a ring.
  4. The ten Sephiroth. The deck is dealt into ten packs laid out as the Tree of Life.

Before the cards are dealt you choose where the significator should fall for the answer to be about your question: the pack, a house and a related one, a sign and a related one, and a Sephira. The form suggests these from your question; the pack’s subjects are Book T’s, and the rest are this site’s suggestion. Book T says to abandon the reading when the significator is not in the pack, or in either house or sign. That happens in most readings, so this site reads on, says so in the prompt, and asks your AI to weigh that operation lightly. A miss in the Sephiroth, Book T says, need not mean the reading has gone astray.

Each pile is read in two passes. Counting starts at the significator, which counts as one, and moves by each card’s number: 4 for Knights, Queens and Princes, 7 for Princesses, 11 for Aces, a small card its own number, and 3, 9 or 12 for a Trump of an element, a planet or a sign. The cards counted make the story. Pairing then takes the cards either side of the significator, then the next two out, and so on, to fill in the detail. The report draws each pile as a ring, with the significator at the top, the count steps numbered and the pairs as lines across it.

Book T doesn’t say where to cut, how to deal, when a count stops, what to do with a card left over from the pairs, or which way each court card faces (the count goes the way the significator looks). This site cuts into equal packs, deals one card to each stack in turn, stops a count at a card already counted, and reads a left-over card on its own. Each court card counts the way its figure looks on the Thoth card, as seen by you: the Knights of Wands, Swords and Disks, the Prince of Wands, the Queens, Prince and Princess of Swords, the Queen of Disks and the Princess of Cups to the right; the Knight, Queen and Prince of Cups and the Prince of Disks to the left. The Queen and Princess of Wands and the Princess of Disks face straight out, and they, like any significator that is not a court card, count from right to left. The prompt says the same.

Birth cards and year cards

An optional extra under More options. It is modern tarot numerology, not Golden Dawn: Mary K. Greer (Tarot for Your Self, 1984) and Angeles Arrien (The Tarot Handbook, 1987, written for the Thoth deck) both teach it.

  • Birth card. Add the month, day and year of your birth as numbers: 14 July 1983 is 7 + 14 + 1983 = 2004. Add the digits, 2 + 0 + 0 + 4 = 6, and keep adding digits while the total is over 22. That Major is your birth card (here VI, The Lovers). A card of 10 or more reduces again to a single digit: 19 is The Sun, then X Fortune, then I The Magus. 22 stands for The Fool.
  • Year card. The same sum with the year of your latest birthday in place of your birth year. It runs from one birthday to the next.

The numbers are the Thoth deck’s own (VIII Adjustment, XI Lust), as Arrien uses them. The prompt names the cards, says whether any of them were drawn, and is told they are context only: they change no card and no score. Your birth date stays in your browser. Only the two card numbers and the year are sent and saved with the reading, and they are left out of share links. The “Numerology” switch under Parts of the AI prompt leaves them out too.

In the AI tests they had a small effect: the AI mentioned them about 1.6 times an answer, and its conclusion shifted a little more than asking again would shift it, though not by enough to be sure on 24 answers. See the ranking.

Worth knowing
  • The interpretation is your AI’s, not this site’s. AIs can be wrong, invent details, or answer the same prompt differently each time. Nothing here checks what they say.
  • One tradition among several. Card attributions differ between systems, which is why there are three to choose from. The dignity scores (+2, +1, −2, 0) and the aspect orbs are this project’s reading of Book T and astrological practice, not the only one. A planetary Major takes the element of one sign its planet rules: the Empress is Earth through Venus’s Taurus, though Venus’s Libra would make her Air, and the Magus, Fortune, the Tower and the Universe make the same kind of choice. The prompt names the sign used on each of these cards.
  • The solids are a modelling choice. Giving the four elements their solids follows Plato’s Timaeus. Giving the planetary Majors the dodecahedron, and naming dual pairs between neighbouring cards, are this project’s own choices, not part of the Golden Dawn tables.
  • The 15-card heap is a variant. It is not the classic First Operation of the Opening of the Key, which splits the whole deck into four piles.
  • The pictures don’t match the Thoth deck. The Thoth paintings are still under copyright, so the cards shown are Pamela Colman Smith’s 1909 public-domain art under the Thoth names. See About the card art.
  • A seed is a repeatable shuffle, not chance. If you want a fresh draw, leave the seed blank or use a new one.

About the card art

The readings use the Thoth deck of Aleister Crowley and Lady Frieda Harris (painted 1938–43). Harris’s paintings are still under copyright, so each card is shown with its counterpart from the Rider-Waite-Smith deck, drawn by Pamela Colman Smith for A. E. Waite in 1909 and now in the public domain. Both decks come from the Golden Dawn’s system, so every Thoth card has a Waite counterpart, though many go by a different name.

Each picture keeps the name Waite printed on it, as a check on the Thoth title above it. Waite printed no names on the numbered cards (2 to 10), so this site adds a banner with the Thoth title there. Court cards

The Golden Dawn named its courts King, Queen, Prince and Princess, with the King on horseback. Waite kept the older playing-card names, King, Queen, Knight and Page. Crowley renamed the Golden Dawn’s mounted King “Knight”. This site pairs the cards by rank and element, as the Golden Dawn did:

The debate

Which Waite court card goes with which Thoth one is still argued over, and the decks’ own books don’t settle it. Waite’s Pictorial Key to the Tarot (1910) simply uses the playing-card names. Crowley’s Book of Thoth (1944) explains that his Knight is the Golden Dawn’s King, renamed. There are two common readings:

  • By rank (used on this site, and the most common): the top card of each suit goes with the top card, and so on down, so the Thoth Knight is the Waite King and the Prince the Waite Knight.
  • By the horse: the Golden Dawn King and Crowley’s Knight both ride horses, like Waite’s Knights. On this reading the Thoth Knight is the Waite Knight and the Thoth Prince the Waite King.

Both readings pair the Queens with each other, and the Princesses with Waite’s Pages. For more, see “Thoth vs Rider court cards” on Tarot Forum, and Michael Sternbach’s “How do the court cards of various decks really relate to one another?”, which argues for the second reading.

What is stored
  • A reading drawn with Draw my cards (on the start page or the Pick page) is saved on the server, with its question, so its report has its own private link (a random address that search engines are told not to list).
  • Shared links (/reading?seed=…), the sample reading and the card of the day are worked out fresh each time and not saved.
  • Your last settings and your theme are remembered in your own browser only.
  • A birth date is never sent or stored. Only the card it gives (a significator, or the birth and year card numbers) is.
  • There are no accounts. The question is optional, so leave out anything you would not want stored.
  • A message sent with the contact form is saved with the time it was sent, and only the site owner reads it. No address, cookie or browser details are kept with it.
